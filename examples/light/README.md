# Where Light Sleeps

An original 27-second paper nocturne, rendered at 1920 × 1080, 30 fps.

A copper moth approaches a dark seed in an abandoned botanical observatory. Its touch lights the seed. Twenty-four spring-driven petals unfold into a celestial flower; escaping stars awaken the dormant garden beneath it.

## Direction

- **Arrival, 0–3.23 s:** the suspended crescent establishes the destination. Cool nested arches frame the small moth.
- **Listening, 3.23–4.93 s:** an arrival-triggered cut brings the moth and seed close. A questioning synthesized chirp breaks the ambience.
- **Contact, about 9.05 s:** the moth's face reaches the seed's left edge. A chime, paper particles and a recoil share this event.
- **Unfolding, 10.55–16.55 s:** the camera withdraws as staggered springs unfurl two rings of engraved petals. A rising harp figure accompanies the opening.
- **Propagation, 16.55–21.55 s:** stars spread through damped attraction, connect into faint constellations, and the background flowers awaken in a wave.
- **Rest, 21.55–27 s:** the moth hovers beside its creation. The title appears; image and sound fade together.

The timings after arrival and contact are observed from the deterministic simulation, not hard-coded contact timestamps.

## Construction

`Moth.ts` combines the engine's `Swimmer`, spring-driven `Skeleton`, separately hinged paper wings, clipped markings, veins and eye details. `main.ts` owns the story-specific flower, observatory, garden, beats, shots and synthesized score. Everything is generated by code; no image or audio assets are used. Existing scene implementations were not consulted.

## Run

```bash
pnpm render light
pnpm grab light 1 5 9.1 12 15 19 24
pnpm sheet light 1 0 27
pnpm typecheck
pnpm test
```

Output: `out/light.mp4` (stereo soundtrack included).
